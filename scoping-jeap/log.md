## 10th September 2026
I considered a few different kinds of review for this project. I thought scoping was the most appropriate because of the mixed genres / paradigms and for the 'quick map' and 'reconnaissance' approach. It was kind of the closest fit rather than a perfect fit. One option I considered was a 'methods' or 'methodology' review, but ultimately I scrapped that because I think they are linked to appraising methods. This doesn't appraise methods and @arkseyScopingStudiesMethodological2005 [] are clear that scoping reviews don't attempt to do so. 

My main concern is this idea that scoping is for getting a read on some new and emerging work or for work that is relatively 'unstudied' and this is not that. I guess the key thing is that I have to justify what **this** scoping review is for **this** project. Research framed like this is not about 'following a formula' but it is about having a coherent argument for your choices.

## 24th September 2026
I took a brief break from the project to visit Oslo with family. On my return I coded Volume 63 of JEAP, published May 2023. From this I have made a number of changes to the extraction form. I noticed that the submitted and accepted dates sometimes had large gaps that I felt was an interesting finding if time to publication is considered a resource. I also found that describing epistemology and methodology didn't capture research that used prior frameworks, so I added a 'framework' field. I have also added a section to allow me to take notes on conceptual coherence. As in the study by @riaziTrustworthinessL2Writing2023, I noticed a number of occasions where language used to warrant quantitative research (reliability, validity, objectivity) was being used for more interpretive work. I felt this was worth recording, although it perhaps stretches the RQs a little. 

To support my coding I consulted Claude.ai to write html code for an ['extraction form' tool](tools/coding_tool.html). The tool works by 'loading' a Zotero export csv into an html page. The html page reads the .csv file, and allows the user (me) to add extracted information and then save a new csv file with the updated details. No data is sent anywhere in the process of extraction. 

I also decided to limit the scope of the research significantly for feasibility reasons. From the pilot, I estimate I can code around one Volume in one day of work. This suggests that, as a Term 1 (Sept > Dec) project, I can only do one year. I have decided to do 2025 only as an initial study, with the option of completing 2024 or 2026 as an extension / comparison if the study goes well. 

I had a 'peer debriefing' with my supervisor about this project, and we discussed the extraction form and the possibility that the ability to pilot studies was also a potential resource. As I have discovered with this project, piloting is a time-consuming but valuable process, so it is relevant. However, I have not worked out if this can be worked into the extraction form. In any case, this was a fruitful discussion, and important when working towards credibility.
